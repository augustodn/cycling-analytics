# Build Plan for XCM 50km Race - April 13 to May 10, 2026

## Race Details
- **Event:** XCM 50km on MTB
- **Race Date:** Sunday, May 10, 2026
- **Format:** 3 laps of the same circuit
- **Terrain:** Mix of singletrack and gravel/rural roads
- **Demands:** Repeated short punchy efforts, frequent re-accelerations, technical pacing, sustained fueling over multiple laps

---

## Block Priorities
1. Recover from the April 12 race without losing sharpness.
2. Improve repeatability of short VO2/above-threshold efforts.
3. Add MTB specificity: cornering, braking, traction, and accelerations after technical sections.
4. Keep overall load close to polarized distribution: most work in Zone 2, two intensity touches per week.
5. Protect the ankle by preferring seated power and avoiding torque-heavy grinding.

---

## Bike Usage Guidelines
- **Smart trainer:** Primary quality sessions where power and cadence can be controlled precisely.
- **Road bike:** Aerobic Zone 2 volume with smooth high cadence.
- **MTB:** Specific race work, technical handling, terrain tolerance, and fueling practice.

Suggested split across the block:
- **Road bike:** ~40-45%
- **Trainer:** ~25-30%
- **MTB:** ~30-35%

In the last two weeks, shift slightly more time toward the MTB.

  - 6 min easy between sets

### Wednesday, April 29
- **Bike:** Road
- **Duration:** 90-120 min
- **Session:** Zone 2 aerobic ride

### Thursday, April 30
- **Bike:** MTB (preferred) or Smart trainer (alternate if short on time)
- **If riding outdoors (MTB) - Duration:** 75-90 min
  - **Session:** Skills plus uphill surges
    - 15 min warm-up
    - 10 x 1 min very hard uphill / 2-3 min easy
    - RPE 9/10, but keep it controlled and seated where possible
    - Finish with 20-30 min of singletrack focusing on exits and re-acceleration
- **Trainer alternative (if short on time) - Duration:** 45-60 min
  - **Session:** Seated uphill-surge simulation
    - 10-15 min easy warm-up (include 3-4 x 15 s high-cadence pickups)
    - 8-10 x (1 min hard / 2 min easy)
    - Target power: ~118-122% FTP for the 1 min efforts (start at the low end if the ankle feels sensitive or if Tuesday left residual fatigue)
    - Easy recoveries at ~50-60% FTP
    - Cadence: keep seated; 75-85 rpm if simulating climbing torque, or 85-95 rpm if you prefer slightly faster seated surges
    - 8-10 min cool-down
  - **How to simulate uphill on the trainer:**
    - Use ERG mode to hit the power targets, or increase resistance/gearing so cadence falls ~10-20 rpm below your Z2 cadence while remaining seated.
    - Avoid long standing efforts; if you feel any ankle discomfort, reduce power 5-10% and focus on cadence control.
  - **Shorter time-crunched option (30-35 min):**
    - 10 min warm-up; 6 x (1 min / 2 min) at ~118-120% FTP; 5-8 min cool-down.
- **Notes:** This trainer alternative is mechanically a bit safer than repeating another 40/20-style set because it reduces the number of hard accelerations while preserving the intended Thursday stimulus. Keep ankle rules in place - prefer seated accelerations and stop or reduce intensity if pain >3/10.

### Friday, May 1

### Saturday, May 2
- **Bike:** MTB
- **Duration:** 2.5-3 h
- **Session:** Race-circuit recon plus one controlled simulation lap
  - 20-30 min easy warm-up before the first meaningful effort if logistics allow
  - **Lap 1: Recon lap**
    - Ride mostly Zone 2 / low Zone 3
    - Focus on lines, braking points, traction, corner exits, and where you naturally surge
    - Identify where eating and drinking are easiest
  - **Lap 2: Simulation lap**
    - Ride at "lap-feel": strong but controlled, about 88-95% FTP overall feel
    - Let short climbs and exits rise naturally above threshold, but avoid repeated all-out spikes
    - Stay seated as much as possible and avoid torque-heavy grinding
    - Think smooth repeatability, not hero numbers
  - **Lap 3: Optional recon/reset lap**
    - Only if ankle, legs, and breathing all feel good
    - Keep it easy Zone 2 and use it to reinforce lines and pacing choices
    - Skip this lap if fatigue is elevated or if the simulation lap already gave enough load
  - If total time is getting too long, prioritize Lap 1 + Lap 2 and skip extra volume
  - If energy or ankle comfort is reduced, shorten the simulation lap to 25-35 min
  - Use race nutrition and hydration exactly as planned for May 10
  - **Fueling execution:**
    - Start intake in the first 15-20 min, not after the simulation starts
    - Aim for 60-90 g carbs/h
    - Drink regularly and use the products you expect to use on race day
  - **Execution cues:**
    - Lap 1 asks: where can you save energy?
    - Lap 2 asks: where should you press and where should you settle?
    - Finish the ride feeling like you could do a little more, not emptied

## Monitoring and Adjustment Rules
- Maintain pain at or below 3/10 during and after rides.
- Monitor next-morning ankle stiffness, especially after Tuesday and Thursday sessions.

- If the ankle reacts badly, reduce Thursday intensity first before cutting aerobic volume.

## Illness-Recovery Adjustments (Apr 19-24)

If you've been unwell since Apr 19 but are feeling noticeably better on Thu Apr 23 and intend to ride on Fri Apr 24, follow this conservative reintroduction so we preserve fitness without provoking a setback.

Immediate rules
- Avoid high-intensity work while you still have systemic symptoms (fever, strong muscle aches, significant cough). If you had fever, wait at least 48 hours after the fever has resolved before doing harder efforts.
- Stop any session immediately for unusual breathlessness, chest pain, dizziness, or palpitations and rest/seek medical advice.
- Use resting heart rate, sleep quality and daytime energy as objective recovery cues: if RHR is elevated >6-8 bpm vs your recent baseline or sleep is poor, reduce/skip the planned session.
- Progress intensity only if you recover fully the day after an easy reintroduction ride.

Weekend microcycle (Apr 24-26) - recommended
- Fri Apr 24 - Easy return ride (if symptom-free and energy reasonable)
  - 45-60 min easy Zone 2: HR 125-140 bpm or RPE 2-3; cadence 90-100 rpm; power ~=55-70% FTP (~150-190 W).
  - Optional: 3-6 x 15-20 s high-cadence, low-torque pickups to test neuromuscular response (not full sprints).
  - Stop if symptoms return or you feel excessive fatigue.
- Sat Apr 25 - Reduced-stress MTB session
  - 90-120 min mostly Zone 2 with skill work.
  - Include at most one short "lap-feel" block (1 x 10-20 min) kept slightly below threshold (~88-95% FTP) if you feel strong. Prefer seated efforts.
  - Practice fueling (60-90 g carbs/hour).
- Sun Apr 26 - Rest (full day)

How to modify the planned build for the next 10-14 days
- Conservative (recommended): reduce intensity and sometimes volume for 7-10 days after illness. Specific changes to Week 3:
  - Tue Apr 28 (trainer punchy repeatability): do 2 sets of 8 x (40 s hard / 20 s easy) at ~120% FTP instead of 2x10 at 125-130%.
  - Thu Apr 30 (MTB uphill surges): reduce to 8-10 x 1 min very hard (or keep 10 reps but target slightly lower power/RPE). Stay mostly seated.
  - Sat May 2 (race simulation): shorten the race-block to 25-35 min if energy is reduced.
- Accelerated (if fully normal for 48+ hours and two easy rides show normal recovery): follow the original Week 3 intensities but monitor next-day recovery closely and back off immediately if you notice elevated RHR, poor sleep, or increased symptoms.

Practical monitoring cues and red flags
- Elevated resting HR, poor sleep, or persistent systemic fatigue the morning after a session: reduce the next day's load by ~30-50% or switch to rest.
- New/worsening chest tightness, palpitations, or breathlessness after returning to exercise: stop high-intensity training for at least 7-10 days and consult a healthcare professional.

Ankle guidance during reintroduction
- Keep the ankle rules in place: prefer seated accelerations, avoid heavy standing torque, and stop or reduce intensity if pain >3/10 or stiffness increases the following morning.
