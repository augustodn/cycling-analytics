# Revised Fueling Framework - Build To XCM November 2026

This file matches:
- `trainings/season_plan_to_xcm_nov2026.md`
- `trainings/winter_gym_strength_plan_2026.md`

This is general guidance and not medical advice. Keep following your physician's or dietitian's recommendations for hemochromatosis.

---

## Main Nutrition Targets
- Support aerobic-base growth now, not aggressive dieting.
- Keep enough carbohydrate around key sessions so FTP work, over-unders, `40/20s`, and long rides stay high quality.
- Practice race fueling early so the November XCM does not become a nutrition experiment.
- Keep protein high enough to support both riding and `2` gym sessions per week.

---

## Daily Targets
- **Protein:** about `1.6-1.8 g/kg/day` = about `120-135 g/day`
- **Base/easier days:** about `3-4 g/kg/day` carbs = about `225-300 g/day`
- **Tuesday hard days:** about `5-7 g/kg/day` carbs = about `375-525 g/day`
- **Saturday long-ride days:** about `6-8 g/kg/day` carbs = about `450-600 g/day`
- **Fat:** fill the remainder, but keep it lower before harder rides

Do not chase low-carb training on Tuesday or Saturday. Those are the sessions that move fitness.

---

## Hemochromatosis Rules
- Avoid high-heme foods such as beef, lamb, liver, shellfish, sardines, and iron supplements unless cleared medically.
- Favor chicken, turkey, white fish, eggs, dairy, and moderate plant proteins.
- Tea or coffee with meals can help reduce iron absorption.
- Avoid large vitamin-C doses with meals that already contain more iron.
- Avoid frequent cast-iron cooking if you are trying to keep iron intake lower.

---

## Weekly Rhythm

### Monday - Gym
- Moderate carbs, high protein.
- Main target: arrive at Tuesday without being underfueled.

### Tuesday - Key Bike Session
- Highest weekday carb target.
- Pre-session meal `2-3 h` before: mostly carbs, low fiber, moderate protein.
- Pre-session snack `30-45 min` before: `20-30 g` carbs.
- During ride:
  - if the hard work is `45-60 min`, water is often enough
  - if total ride time is `75+ min`, take `30-45 g/h`
- Post-session in first hour: `25-30 g` protein plus `60-90 g` carbs.

### Wednesday - Zone 2
- Moderate carb day.
- If ride is `90+ min`, eat a small carb snack before and consider `20-30 g/h` during if you want better recovery for Thursday and Saturday.

### Thursday - Endurance Or MTB Skills
- If it is an easy endurance day, keep intake moderate.
- If it is a race-specific day later in the season, fuel it like a lighter Tuesday.

### Friday - Gym Support Day
- Moderate carbs, high protein.
- If Saturday is a `3 h+` ride or race simulation, increase carbs Friday evening.

### Saturday - Long Ride
- This is the main fueling rehearsal day.
- Pre-ride meal `2.5-3 h` before: large carb meal, moderate protein, low fiber/fat.
- Pre-ride snack `30-45 min` before: `20-30 g` carbs.
- During ride:
  - `2-2.5 h`: `40-60 g/h`
  - `2.5-4.5 h`: `60-90 g/h`
- Start intake in the first `20 min`, not when fatigue starts.
- Post-ride in first hour: `25-30 g` protein plus `1-1.2 g/kg` carbs if legs are depleted.

### Sunday - Rest
- Lower carb intake is fine, but keep protein high.
- Use this day to rehydrate and normalize appetite before the next week.

## Gut Training Progression
- **May to June:** make `40-60 g/h` feel easy on longer rides.
- **July to August:** progress toward `60-75 g/h` on key Saturday rides.
- **September to October:** rehearse `60-90 g/h` during races and simulations.
- **Late October to November:** use the exact carb range and products planned for the main race.

The gut needs training too. Do not wait until November to practice higher carb intake.

---

## Hydration And Sodium
- Use thirst plus conditions as your guide, but rehearse a rough range of about `500-750 ml/h` in moderate conditions.
- In hotter rides or when sweat loss is clearly high, this may rise toward `750-1000 ml/h`.
- Use a sodium target of about `300-600 mg/h` as a practical starting point, then adjust based on sweat rate, heat, and how salty you sweat.
- Test this on long Saturday rides, not on race day.

---

## Phase Alignment

### May To August - Base And FTP Build
- Fuel Tuesday and Saturday aggressively.
- Do not underfuel because the work looks mostly aerobic; that is when durable fitness is built.
- Keep body composition changes slow and non-disruptive.

### September To October - Short-Race Block
- Use the shorter races to practice pre-race breakfast, bottle mix, and on-bike carb timing.
- For races around `1 h 30 min` to `2 h`, aim for `40-75 g/h` depending on tolerance.
- In race week, increase carbs the day before rather than eating heavily only at breakfast.

### Late October To November - XCM Build And Peak
- Practice full XCM fueling every Saturday.
- Aim to make `60-90 g/h` feel normal before race month.
- Use the exact products you trust by the final `2-3` weeks.

---

## Mid-November XCM Fueling Outline
- **Race duration target:** about `3 h 30 min`
- **36-48 h before:** increase carbs clearly, reduce unnecessary fiber, and stay well hydrated.
- **Race breakfast:** carb-rich, low fiber, familiar foods, about `3 h` before start.
- **15 min before start:** optional small carb top-up.
- **During race:** aim for `60-90 g/h`, starting in the first `15-20 min`.
- **Hydration:** drink steadily rather than in large irregular doses.
- **Sodium:** use the tested range from your long rides.
- **Caffeine:** only if already tested in training.

---

## Simple Food Pattern
- **Main carb staples:** white rice, pasta, potatoes, bread, oats, tortillas, bananas, rice cakes
- **Protein staples:** chicken, turkey, merluza, trout, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese
- **Portable ride carbs:** drink mix, bananas, chews, gels, rice cakes with honey
- **Recovery staples:** chocolate milk, yogurt plus oats, bread plus jam, whey plus banana

---

## Non-Negotiables
- Fuel every Tuesday quality session.
- Fuel every Saturday long ride.
- Take carbs after `40/20s`, over-unders, long rides, and races.
- Practice hydration and sodium, not only carbs.
- Do not test new products in the last `10-14` days before the main race.
- Keep nutrition simple enough to repeat every week.
